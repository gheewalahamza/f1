THE CIRCUITS

Formula 1 circuits are specialized closed-loop tracks designed for high-speed motorsport racing. They include traditional road tracks like Monaco, modern purpose-built Hermann Tilke designs like COTA, and high-speed temporary street circuits like Las Vegas, featuring long straightaways, heavy-braking zones, and tight technical corners.

MONTE CARLO CIRCUIT [ROYAL GARDEN]

  • The track length is only 3.337 km (2.074 miles) with 19 sharp turns, leaving no room for error.

  • It features the Grand Hotel Hairpin, the slowest corner in F1, where drivers take the turn at nearly walking speed.

  • Drivers speed through a dark, underground tunnel at over 280 km/h (174 mph), creating a massive shift in light and grip.

  • The track is so narrow that passing other cars is very hard, making Saturday's qualifying session more important than the race itself.

  • First raced in 1929, it is a core part of motorsport and one of the oldest events still on the F1 calendar

BRITISH CIRCUIT [SILVERSTONE]

  • It hosted the very first official Formula 1 World Championship Grand Prix on May 13, 1950.

  • The track's wide, high-speed layout stems from its past life as an RAF bomber airfield operational during World War II

  • Measuring 5.891 km, the modern configuration features legendary, high-load cornering sequences like Copse and the rapid Maggots-Becketts-Chapel complex.

  • It regularly attracts enormous crowds, setting premier attendance records for modern Formula 1 race weekends.

  • British driver Lewis Hamilton holds the record for the most victories at the venue, with nine career wins.

BELGIAN CIRCUIT [SPA-FRANCORCHAMP]

  • At 7.004 km (4.352 miles), Spa-Francorchamps is the longest circuit currently used in the Formula 1 World Championship.

  • This iconic, high-speed left-right-left combination plunges downhill before sweeping steeply uphill, testing driver bravery and car compression limits.

  • Situated in rolling hills and forests, the track is famous for unpredictable weather where one part of the circuit can be soaking wet while another remains dry.

  • Opened in 1921 as a 15 km layout utilizing public roads, it was shortened and redesigned into a safer, permanent facility in 1979.

  • Alongside Formula 1, it hosts legendary endurance tests like the prestigious 24 Hours of Spa.

MIAMI CIRCUIT [AUTODROME]

  • Set in the Hard Rock Stadium complex in Miami Gardens (home of the NFL's Miami Dolphins), the counterclockwise track weaves through surrounding property and parking lots.

  • Features three designated DRS zones, including a massive 1.3-kilometer back straight where cars reach peak velocities near 320–350 km/

  • Turn 11 (end of the shorter straight) and Turn 17 (end of the long back straight) serve as the heaviest braking zones and primary places for passing.

  • Sector 2 contains a tight, winding section—including an uphill/downhill chicane at Turns 14–15 passing over/under highway exit ramps—demanding high precision.

  • Organizers constructed the layout so fans can view the entire track, including unique vantage points from the upper decks of the Hard Rock Stadium.

JAPANESE CIRCUIT [SUZUKA]

  • Suzuka is one of the very few professional tracks in the world with a figure-eight configuration, featuring an overpass where the back straight crosses above the front section.

  • The track was originally commissioned by Honda founder Soichiro Honda to serve as a test facility for company vehicles under his philosophy that "without racing, cars will not improve."

  • It contains world-renowned challenges like the rapid-fire S Curves, the tricky double-apex Degner Curves, the punishing Spoon Curve, and the blistering high-speed 130R corner.

  • Formula 1 first raced here in 1987, and the track has since been the historic site of numerous dramatic world championship deciders and legendary driver duels.

  • Suzuka is widely celebrated for its incredibly enthusiastic, knowledgeable, and creative local fans who fill the grandstands wearing elaborate team merchandise.

ITALIAN CIRCUIT [MONZA]

  • Known for very long straightaways. Cars spend around 80 percent of the lap at full throttle.

  • Built in 1922. It is one of the oldest motor racing tracks in the world.

  • Measures 5.793 kilometers (3.6 miles) long. It features just 11 total corners.

  • Teams use very small, thin wings on the cars. This cuts drag and increases top speeds on straight sections.

  • Famous for passionate local fans. Massive crowds of Ferrari supporters create a sea of red and loud celebrations.
