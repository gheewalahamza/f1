THE BEGINNING

The Formula One World Championship officially began on May 13, 1950, with the British Grand Prix at Silverstone. The international auto racing organization, FIA, created the new rules to unify pre-war Grand Prix racing into one global points-paying series. Alfa Romeo dominated the very first season.

1950[first f1 race]

  • The first formula 1 race was done on the silverstone circuit in britain in 1950.

  • Italian driver Giuseppe "Nino" Farina won the race driving an Alfa Romeo 158.

  • King George VI, Queen Elizabeth, and Princess Margaret attended the event. It remains the only time a reigning British monarch has attended a motor racing event.

  • An estimated 120,000 spectators watched the inaugural event live at the airfield-turned-circuit.

  • Twenty-one drivers started the main Grand Prix, featuring an eclectic mix of professionals and aristocratic entries, including Thai Prince Bira and Swiss Baron de Graffenried.



1958[first constrctor championship]

  • The first constructor championship was introduced in 1958.

  • the first team to win it was british vanwall

  • Despite winning the team title, Vanwall missed the Drivers' Championship, which went to Ferrari driver Mike Hawthorn by a single point.

  • Only the highest-finishing car from each manufacturer earned points in a given race.




1970s[Rise of aerodynamics]

  • Teams mounted elevated front and rear wings to create downward force (downforce).

  • More downforce pushed tires into the asphalt, allowing much higher cornering speeds.

  • Known as the "black beauty," it was the first ground-effect car to utterly dominate and win a Drivers' World Championship (with Mario Andretti).

  • Cars generated huge downforce with minimal extra drag, making them dramatically faster through turns than anything before.




1980s[Turbo era]

  • Boost arrived abruptly like a "light switch," making throttle application treacherous mid-corner.

  • Special one-lap units ran extreme boost pressures and exotic materials, built to survive only a few miles before melting or exploding.

  • A black-and-gold rocket ship that allowed Ayrton Senna to claim staggering pole positions through sheer qualifying pace.

  • The 1986 season marked the absolute peak of pure turbo dominance where every single entry on the grid ran a turbocharged power unit.




2014[Hybrid Era]

  • 1.6-liter V6 single-turbo internal combustion engine combined with an electrical energy recovery system.

  • Maximum fuel flow restricted to 100 kg per hour, with total race consumption capped at 100 kg.

  • Mercedes accurately anticipated the rule change and built a superior power unit, winning an unprecedented eight consecutive Constructors' Championships from 2014 to 2021.

  • Lewis Hamilton claimed six of his drivers' titles during this initial span (2014, 2015, 2017–2020), with teammate Nico Rosberg winning the 2016 championship.




2022[Hybrid Era]

  • The underfloor was redesigned with full-length tunnels to create suction, shifting downforce generation beneath the car and drastically cutting turbulent "dirty air" behind it.

  • Complex bargeboards were banned, and front and rear wings were streamlined to direct airflow cleanly over following cars.

  • The budget cap was reduced to $140 million for the season to level the playing field among teams.

  • introduced a radical overhaul of technical, sporting, and financial regulations designed to improve wheel-to-wheel racing, limit costs, and make the sport more sustainable.
